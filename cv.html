04PhysioFix architectureTwo planes: audio (Twilio + Grok) and control (Express). Walk one call.~67s✓

Walk me through the PhysioFix architecture.

There are two planes. The audio plane is Twilio and Grok voice: the clinic number routes the call over a SIP trunk to Grok, and the voice stays there. The control plane is mine.

When a call comes in, xAI sends a webhook to my Express server. I verify it against the shared secret, return 200 right away, and then open a realtime socket for that call. On the socket I send the session instructions, including the caller's record from Firestore if they have called before. Then every event for that call runs through one queue: tool calls, transcript lines, and the hangup. Tool calls are acked immediately and the Firestore writes happen after. When the socket closes, I archive the recording.

The Next.js admin reads the same Firestore data. Cloud Build deploys the voice process to a GCE VM over HTTPS with restart on failure, and to Cloud Run. The admin deploys separately to Cloud Run.

Có hai lớp. Lớp âm thanh là Twilio và Grok voice: số của phòng khám chuyển cuộc gọi qua SIP trunk sang Grok, và giọng nói nằm ở đó. Lớp điều khiển là phần tôi làm.

06Hardest problem on PhysioFixOrdering and latency on a live call: one queue, ack first, write after, polite hangup.~66s✓

What was the hardest problem on PhysioFix, and how did you solve it?

Timing on a live call. Grok can send a batch of tool calls, and the caller keeps talking while I handle them. If every event is handled as soon as it arrives, a slow tool result can land after the next utterance has started, and the conversation goes out of order.

So every event for a call goes through one queue and runs in order. A tool batch finishes before the next utterance is handled, so they cannot race.

The queue also has to stay fast, or the caller hears silence. So I ack each tool call on the socket immediately, and the contact and transcript writes to Firestore happen afterward. Grok keeps speaking while the database catches up.

The last piece was the end of the call. If I close the session the moment Grok says goodbye, the caller loses the last sentence. So hangup waits until the last spoken line can finish.

Vấn đề khó nhất ở PhysioFix là gì, bạn giải quyết thế nào?

Là thời gian trong một cuộc gọi đang diễn ra. Grok có thể gửi một loạt tool call, trong khi người gọi vẫn tiếp tục nói. Nếu sự kiện nào đến là xử lý ngay, một kết quả tool chậm có thể về sau khi câu nói tiếp theo đã bắt đầu, và cuộc hội thoại bị lộn thứ tự.

Vì vậy mọi sự kiện của một cuộc gọi đi qua một hàng đợi và chạy lần lượt. Loạt tool call xong thì mới đến câu nói tiếp theo, nên không còn tranh nhau.

Hàng đợi cũng phải nhanh, nếu không người gọi sẽ nghe im lặng. Nên tôi ack từng tool call trên socket ngay lập tức, còn việc ghi liên hệ và transcript vào Firestore làm sau. Grok vẫn nói tiếp trong lúc database ghi xong.

Phần cuối là lúc kết thúc cuộc gọi. Nếu tôi đóng phiên ngay khi Grok nói lời chào, người gọi sẽ mất câu cuối. Nên lệnh cúp máy chờ đến khi câu nói cuối cùng nói xong.

08PhysioFix: what you'd do differentlyDurable writes, automatic drift alert, replayed call tests.~54s✓

Looking back, what would you do differently on PhysioFix?

Three things. First, the Firestore write happens after the ack. That keeps the call fluent, but it makes that write the one place a contact could be lost. I'd put those writes behind a small durable outbox with retries, so a failure is retried and visible.

Second, the trunk drift check shows the difference to a human, who resyncs by hand. That was the right first step, but I'd add a scheduled check that alerts when the stored flag and Twilio disagree, so nobody has to open the screen to find out.

Third, I'd record a few real event sequences from calls, with tool batches and interruptions, and replay them in Jest. Then ordering bugs show up in a test run, not on a patient's call.

Nhìn lại, ở PhysioFix bạn sẽ làm khác điều gì?

Ba điều. Thứ nhất, việc ghi Firestore diễn ra sau khi ack. Cách đó giữ cuộc gọi trôi chảy, nhưng cũng khiến bước ghi đó là chỗ duy nhất có thể mất một liên hệ. Tôi sẽ đưa các lần ghi đó qua một outbox bền, có retry, để lỗi được thử lại và nhìn thấy được.

09Why return 200 before opening the socket?The webhook has a deadline; the socket and Firestore reads do not belong in it.~56s✓

Why does your webhook handler return 200 before it opens the realtime socket?

Because the webhook has a deadline and the slow work doesn't belong inside it. A caller is waiting on the line, and the sender of a webhook expects a fast answer. If my handler opened the socket, loaded the caller from Firestore, and sent instructions before responding, one slow step could turn into a timeout, and a timeout can mean a retry, which means a second session for the same call.

So the order is fixed. Check the secret first, and return 401 if it's wrong, so nobody can make my server open sockets by posting to that URL. Then return 200 immediately. Then do the slow work for that call. If something fails after the 200, the sender won't tell me, so I log it against the call ID.

Tại sao handler webhook trả 200 trước rồi mới mở realtime socket?

Vì webhook có thời hạn, còn phần việc chậm thì không nên nằm trong đó. Người gọi đang chờ máy, và bên gửi webhook muốn có trả lời nhanh. Nếu handler mở socket, đọc người gọi từ Firestore, gửi hướng dẫn xong mới trả lời, chỉ một bước chậm là có thể timeout, mà timeout có thể dẫn đến gửi lại, tức là thêm một phiên nữa cho cùng một cuộc gọi.

Nên thứ tự là cố định. Kiểm tra secret trước, sai thì trả 401, để không ai có thể bắt server mở socket chỉ bằng cách gửi request vào URL đó. Rồi trả 200 ngay. Rồi mới làm phần việc chậm cho cuộc gọi đó. Nếu có lỗi sau khi đã trả 200, bên gửi sẽ không báo cho tôi, nên tôi log lỗi theo call ID.

Know: General webhook rule: verify, ack fast, work after. More on retries and idempotency: Webhook retries.

Trap: On Cloud Run, work after the response needs CPU that stays allocated (instance-based billing); with request-based billing the CPU is throttled once the response is sent. That is part of why the voice process also runs on a VM.

Follow-up: "What if the same webhook arrives twice?" → key the session by call ID and ignore a second open for the same call.

10The switch to a human, and trunk driftTwilio is the source of truth; the Firestore flag is only intent.~57s✓

How does the switch between the assistant and staff work? What do you mean by drift?

The admin has a switch that takes the Twilio number off the Grok trunk and dials a staff phone instead, and back again. The important part is that the switch changes the real thing, Twilio trunk membership, not only a flag in Firestore.

But the two can still drift apart. Someone can change the number in the Twilio console, or the call to Twilio can fail after the flag is saved. If the admin trusted the flag, staff would see "assistant on" while calls went somewhere else.

So the screen reads the live trunk membership, compares it with the stored flag, and shows a warning when they disagree, with a way to resync from Twilio. Twilio is the source of truth. The flag is our record of what we meant to do.

16Hardest problem on Aquanix: the nightly jobProcess the ids you just promoted; do not re-query for them.~53s✓

What was the hardest problem on Aquanix?

The trickiest one was the nightly HanoiCheck job. It has two steps: promote the queued batches, then process them. The natural way to write the second step is a collection query for the promoted status. But that query could miss writes that had only just landed in step one, so a batch promoted tonight might wait until the next night.

The fix was to stop depending on the re-query. Step one returns the ids it promoted, and the same request processes exactly those ids. Nothing has to be found again, so nothing can be missed.

The general lesson I took from it: when one step already knows which records it changed, pass those ids forward instead of asking the database to find them again.

47A production incident you handledDisk full on a Linux host: confirm, free safely, restart, verify, write it down.~53s✓

Tell me about a production incident you handled.

At Akktis the incident I handled most on Linux hosts was a full disk. A site suddenly fails, and the cause is logs or uploads filling the volume.

I confirm it first: df to see which filesystem is full, du to find what grew. Then I free space safely: rotate or compress the logs and clear only what I know is safe. I never delete blindly under /var. Then I restart the affected service and check that the site actually answers, not just that the process is running. Last, I write down what to watch so the next one is caught early.

That's my pattern for any incident: confirm the cause, apply the smallest fix, verify from the user's side, and write it down.
